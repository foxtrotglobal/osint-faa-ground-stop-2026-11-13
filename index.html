FAA Nationwide Ground Stop — 13 November 2026

Open-Source Intelligence Desk  |  Information cut-off: 3:40 PM CT, Fri 13 Nov 2026  |  Classification: UNCLASSIFIED // OPEN SOURCE

VERDICT: CONFIRMED and CURRENT. This is not a recycled story. The FAA Command Center issued ATCSCC Advisory 111, “NATIONAL GROUND STOP,” signed 20:54Z (2:54 PM CT) today. It orders that “ALL AIRPORTS ARE GROUND STOPPED. ALL DEPARTING TRAFFIC SHOULD REMAIN GROUND STOPPED UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE.”[1] The Transportation Secretary and @FAANews both confirmed it.[5][6][11] One detail is not established: the claim that it was caused by “frequency issues.” Officials say only that there was a “communication issue.” The “frequency” detail comes from credible reporting and industry messages, not from an official finding.[5][9][10]

1. Key judgments

  • Confirmed: All U.S. departures are held. Airborne flights may continue to land. Inbound international flights are handled case by case.[1][8] The Air Current reports the message to airlines covered “the U.S. and Canada.”[9]
  • Confirmed (official wording): the stop followed “a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers,” and FAA technicians are investigating.[5] An FAA statement quoted by Axios says “multiple” centers rather than “all.”[7] No official root cause has been given.
  • Credible reporting:
    • The FAA's message to airlines cited “several impacts to ATC Frequencies and/or Radar capabilities” and said the Command Center had no information on the “timing or cause.”[9][25]
    • Two FAA employees described the problem to the Washington Examiner as a “rolling frequency outage.”[10][45]
    • American Airlines told customers it was a “technical issue affecting telecommunications at many air traffic control facilities.”[16]
  • Most likely explanation (assessed): a failure in the telecom or radio infrastructure that links ARTCCs to their remote radio and radar sites. We have no open-source evidence of a cyberattack, jamming, staffing problem, or security incident, including hijacking. None of these can be formally ruled out until the FAA gives a cause.
  • Expected duration: the advisory's event window runs from 2:53 PM to 10:00 PM CT, and the effective time runs to 10:30 PM CT.[1] That matches Flighty's report that the stop was “extended 7 hours”[23] and News9's report of an estimated end around 10 PM CT.[15] It may lift earlier or be extended.

3. Scope

  • Departures: all U.S. airports.[1] The ORD and MDW stops list every CONUS center, including ZFW (Fort Worth) and ZHU (Houston), plus some Canadian airports.[4]
  • Airborne aircraft: may continue to their destinations.[8][15] No order to land at the nearest airport has been reported, a key difference from 9/11 (§4).[32]
  • International inbound: case by case.[1]
  • Facilities named: officials say “all” or “multiple” ARTCCs.[5][7] Unverified chatter also names Chicago, New York, Boston and Kansas City Centers.[24][43]

On the “extraordinary circumstances” wording: it is the Command Center's catch-all phrase for “by order of the Command Center.” It does not by itself signal a security event. The FAA's own statement to the media paired it with a communications cause.[1][7]

5. Current status: one discrepancy to flag

The NAS Status board snapshot at 3:29 PM CT does not list a national ground stop.[4] The BBC reports that the alert was removed.[14] Many X replies, including automated AI replies, claim the stop is “lifted.”[44] However:

  • Advisory 111 was still retrievable and uncancelled on the ATCSCC site at about 3:36 PM CT.[1]
  • Flighty reported an extension at 3:22 PM CT.[23]
  • Airports were still holding departures at 3:26 PM CT.[17][18]

Assessment: treat the stop as still in effect until the FAA, the Secretary, or an ATCSCC cancellation advisory says otherwise. A “lifted” claim is unverified.

7. Prior comparable incidents

  • Sept 11, 2001: the Command Center ordered a nationwide ground stop at 9:25 ET, then at 9:42 ordered all aircraft to land. About 4,500 aircraft landed.[32]
  • Jan 11, 2023: a NOTAM system failure caused a domestic departure stop of under 2 hours. It was the only all-departures national stop between 9/11 and today.[8][15]
  • May 17, 1995: a computer outage at Chicago Center led to a national ground stop for traffic headed into Chicago airspace.[33]
  • Apr 28, 2025: at Newark/Philadelphia TRACON, a failed telecom line caused a radar and radio blackout of up to 90 seconds.[34][35]
  • Context: Congress approved $12.5B for ATC communications last year. The FAA and White House asked for another $30.1B today.[8]

8. Impact: DFW, DAL and Houston

  • DFW (American's hub), Love Field (Southwest), IAH (United's hub) and Hobby are all affected. Love Field and IAH tell passengers to check with their airlines.[17][18]
  • If the stop runs to the 10:00–10:30 PM CT window,[1] expect most Friday evening departures to be cancelled, crews to time out, and knock-on disruption into Saturday morning. (Assessment.)
  • Don't go to the airport without confirmation that your flight is operating. Rebook in the airline app, and watch for fee waivers. For DFW–Houston trips, driving is a realistic fallback. (Advice.)

9. Indicators to watch

  • An ATCSCC cancellation or replacement advisory for Advisory 111, and a restart plan (staged releases by center).
  • A FAA or DOT root-cause statement naming equipment, telecom or vendor. Any NATCA statement on controller safety.
  • Any CISA, FBI or DHS statement. Its absence keeps the cyber and security hypotheses low.
  • Any order for airborne aircraft to land, any SCATANA or security-control declaration, any 7500 reports, or any NORAD activity. Any of these would sharply raise Hypothesis F.
  • Airline waivers and FlightAware cancellation counts at DFW, DAL and IAH. Congressional reaction tied to the $30.1B ATC funding request.
